# UI/UX guide

Record the audience, the task they came to do, the screens in scope and any device or accessibility requirements. Use the project's brief, existing design system and supplied references. Ask a focused question if the answer would materially change the design. Otherwise, record your assumption and move ahead. Implement when the user asks for a build.

## Layout

Make the primary task easy to find and keep controls close to the content they affect. Give the most important information the clearest hierarchy. Use layout, spacing and typography to show which items belong together.

When users move between a list or overview and an item, keep their selection and context visible. Decide what should stay in view on small screens and make it easy to return to the previous view.

Choose how to present information based on what users need to understand or compare. Include units, time ranges and baselines where they affect interpretation.

## Visual direction

Use the project's design system when one exists. Otherwise, define the colours, typography, spacing and component styles that fit the product and audience. Check contrast, readability and consistency at the intended screen sizes.

Use copy, imagery and motion where they help someone understand the interface, navigate it or complete a task.

## Interactions and states

Specify what happens when a user focuses, selects or activates a control. Make focus and selection visible and give keyboard and touch users access to the same actions. Respect reduced-motion preferences and keep feedback understandable with motion disabled.

Cover the default, loading, empty, error and recovery states for the main flow. Include disabled, stale or permission-limited states when applicable. Show what happened and what the user can do next. Use a label or icon alongside colour when conveying status.

For data products, show sources and freshness where they matter. Label estimates, simulations and proposed changes. Keep a preview distinct from a confirmed result.

## Handoff

Provide the screen hierarchy, primary flow, relevant states, small-screen behaviour and the visual rules needed to reproduce the design. Explain what you drew from any references you used. Include a checklist for legibility, keyboard focus, touch, reduced motion and realistic content.
